\begin{center}
    \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

The artificial intelligence (AI) model Coconet can harmonize a soprano melody in four parts. Huang et al.'s (2019) Bach Doodle analysis identified parallel fifths and octaves in Coconet outputs, while Gustav Strube's harmony textbook provides voice-leading guidance with conditions and exceptions. This study is designed as an evaluative case study using a qualitative approach to one melody from Strube's chorale harmonization chapter. MODEL no.~285 is the primary candidate, subject to verification of its complete transcription. The soprano is retained as Coconet’s fixed input, and the printed harmonization provides a reference for voice-leading choices. Twenty generation attempts are planned, retaining failures and identical outputs. A music21-based program assists in locating candidate events; the researcher then reviews every eligible opportunity included in the counts and examines the score context to distinguish confirmed deviations, events accepted under stated conditions or exceptions, and unresolved decisions. The analysis covers melodic movement in the alto, tenor, and bass, relationships between voices, and hidden-interval patterns as a supplementary profile. Rule-specific decisions and patterns across outputs are explained through score analysis, supported by counts and durations. The study seeks to explain which features of AI harmonizations require inspection under Strube's guidance. Conclusions are limited to the tested case, checkpoint, and settings, without measuring listener perception or users' learning outcomes.\par

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Automatic Harmonization, Voice-Leading Rules, Gustav Strube, Chorale, Coconet
