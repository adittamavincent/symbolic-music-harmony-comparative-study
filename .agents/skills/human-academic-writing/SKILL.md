# Human academic writing

Write prose the researcher can understand, own, and defend. Aim for clear, specific, well-supported academic writing with the researcher's cadence. Calling a text human is an editorial aim, not a claim about its authorship or a measurable outcome established by this skill.

StoryScope finds differences in narrative decisions that persist after surface edits. Removing conspicuous words or punctuation is therefore an incomplete editing strategy. Its fiction classifier does not establish how to write a good Indonesian thesis. The adaptations below are editorial hypotheses, not validated transfers of its measurements.

## Establish the material

Read the complete supplied draft before editing. For a file edit, read the entire affected section and its neighboring paragraphs; read the chapter when the section depends on its larger argument. Identify the point, reader, and a few voice signals internally: ordinary vocabulary, explanatory detail, cadence, directness, or useful uncertainty. Do not infer a distinctive personal voice solely from prose previously generated by agents.

Draft only from supplied material or verified project sources. For a new paragraph, first identify its job, supporting facts, and what it adds to the surrounding argument. This is an internal planning aid, not a fixed sentence template or material to insert into the thesis. If a needed fact or intended meaning is missing, edit the supported material and ask a focused question about the gap.

## Make decisions before polishing

1. **Trace the claims.** Separate source statements, researcher choices, operational definitions, implementation behavior, plans, and measured results. Keep the citation and scope attached to the relevant statement.
2. **Check the argument.** Remove repeated significance claims and conclusions that close a question the evidence leaves open. Preserve required definitions, reasoning, comparisons, exceptions, and uncertainty. Explain the actual inferential step when the reader needs it.
3. **Check the paragraph's contribution.** It should introduce, define, support, compare, qualify, or connect something specific. A paragraph may do several related jobs. Do not force every paragraph into claim-evidence-summary form.
4. **Edit the language.** Fix Indonesian syntax, unclear referents, punctuation, wordiness, and nominalizations. Use direct verbs and concrete subjects where clearer; retain useful passive voice in methods. Keep technical terms stable.
5. **Read across boundaries.** Check antecedents, terminology, tense, denominators, transitions, and repeated explanations against neighboring paragraphs and the research questions.

Make the minimum effective edit. Structural revision is warranted when a real gap, repetition, or unsupported inference requires it; deleting a few banned words will not resolve those problems. Leave clear, accurate sentences alone.

## Boundaries that matter in this thesis

- Preserve names, numbers, units, statistical qualifications, citation keys and page arguments, formulas, labels, and references. Do not move a citation so it appears to support a new claim. If an authorized substantive correction changes any of these, explain it and retain its evidence.
- Do not turn planned generations into collected data, software checks into instrument validation, or a model comparison into an architectural cause. Do not replace a narrow metric with a general claim about musical quality.
- Do not manufacture experiences, personal judgments, observations from listening, source pages, supervisor approval, literature gaps, or disagreement to make prose sound human. The researcher's actual choices and limits provide specificity.
- Preserve useful epistemic qualifiers such as “pada sampel ini,” “menurut definisi operasional,” and “di antara sumber yang ditinjau.” Delete an empty hedge only when its removal preserves claim strength.
- Explain why a limitation constrains the conclusion. Do not append the same stock disclaimer to every paragraph.
- Preserve the project's choice of `bas`, `gerak suara`, and the applicable distinction between `pelanggaran per birama` and `pelanggaran per kesempatan`, unless the user requests a terminology change. Consult current sources for other terms.
- Protect LaTeX syntax, comments, and unaffected formatting. Do not edit front matter or proposal milestones as a side effect. Track readiness outside the manuscript, in the existing project documentation.
- Avoid generic openings, synonym cycling, decorative metaphors, dramatic contrasts, colon reveals, interpretive puffery, and recap endings. Do not make sentences choppy, add slang, or introduce deliberate errors as a substitute for voice.
- Do not import fiction's flashbacks, suspense, moral ambiguity, dialogue, sensory detail, or direct reader address as thesis requirements. Academic reasoning and necessary explanation stay explicit.

## Modes and delivery

**Draft or edit:** return the edited passage and a short **What changed** explanation. For direct file edits, the saved passage is the full deliverable; link it and summarize substantive changes instead of pasting a chapter unless requested. State any unresolved evidence issue outside the manuscript.

**Detect or audit:** do not rewrite. Name the observable pattern, quote the affected line, locate it, and give a brief fix. Include structural patterns as well as language patterns. Report consequential gaps without assigning an AI probability, authorship label, or “human score.”

Before delivery, check every item in `docs/final-thesis/eval.md`, compare original and revised claim strength, and reread the edited passage with its neighbors. Stop when the requested scope is clear, supported, grammatically sound, and connected; repeated polishing can erase the voice. A passed checklist is editorial verification, not evidence that this skill improves writing statistically.
